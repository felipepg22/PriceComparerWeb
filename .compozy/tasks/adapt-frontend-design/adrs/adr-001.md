# ADR-001: Prioritize Comparison Dashboard Experience

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-17

## Context

The frontend needs to adapt the existing product search experience to the visual direction in `client/DESIGN.md`. The current app already supports a search form, result metrics, offer cards, confidence values, source names, and partial-failure states. The product direction selected for the PRD is to make result analysis and fast offer scanning the center of the experience for everyday shoppers.

The redesign must improve trust through clearer offer details, source transparency, and visual hierarchy. It must retain offer count, candidate pages, attempted sources, and confidence information, but it should not show failed source details in the main experience.

## Decision

Use a comparison dashboard product direction. The redesigned frontend will make the search-and-results workspace the primary experience, with a polished Wise-inspired surface, strong comparison hierarchy, visible source and confidence signals, and scan-friendly offer rows or cards.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Trust-first landing/search hybrid

- **Description**: Lead with brand credibility and a prominent search card before exposing results.
- **Pros**: Welcoming for new users, strong first impression, clear alignment with the design reference.
- **Cons**: Gives less first-screen weight to comparison analysis and repeat-use workflows.
- **Why rejected**: The selected direction prioritizes fast result analysis over broader landing-page persuasion.

### Alternative 2: Savings-first marketing/search page

- **Description**: Emphasize speed and savings with stronger value messaging before the search.
- **Pros**: Clear consumer value proposition and simple acquisition story.
- **Cons**: Makes source transparency and confidence feel secondary.
- **Why rejected**: The success criterion is trust through offer details, source transparency, and visual hierarchy, not just speed or savings.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Everyday shoppers can evaluate offers faster without opening many tabs.
- The product experience better communicates source transparency and confidence.
- The visual refresh can stay focused on the existing search and comparison workflow.

### Negative

- The first screen may feel less like a broad marketing landing page.
- New users will rely on concise dashboard copy rather than a longer explanatory narrative.

### Risks

- The dashboard could become visually dense if every metric competes for attention. Mitigate by making price, seller/source, and confidence the primary scan path.
- Hiding failed source details could reduce diagnostic transparency for power users. Mitigate by preserving high-level attempted-source context without surfacing a failure list by default.

## Implementation Notes

The TechSpec should determine the exact layout and component structure. The PRD should stay focused on user capabilities, visual outcomes, and business value.
